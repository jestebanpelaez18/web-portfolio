summary:
  - "Cub3D draws a 3D-looking maze from a flat 2D map, the technique used by early first-person games. It is written in C, with MLX42 for the window, keyboard input and textures."
  - "For every column of the screen, the engine casts a ray from the player, finds the first wall it hits and draws a vertical slice of wall whose height depends on the distance."
highlights:
  - title: "DDA raycasting"
    text: "Rays step through the map grid square by square with the DDA algorithm, so finding the wall takes only a few steps per column."
  - title: "No fisheye"
    text: "Wall height uses the perpendicular distance to the camera plane instead of the straight-line distance, which keeps walls straight at the edges of the screen."
  - title: "Textured walls"
    text: "Each wall side (north, south, east and west) has its own texture, and the exact point where the ray hits the wall picks the column of the texture to draw."
  - title: "A strict map parser"
    text: "The .cub file is parsed and validated before anything renders: texture paths, floor and ceiling colours, and a map fully closed by walls."
